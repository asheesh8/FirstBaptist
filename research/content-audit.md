# First Congregational Church of St. Albans - content and source audit

Research snapshot: August 18, 2026. This document separates verified source material from recommendations. No website build or paid asset generation has begun.

## Executive finding

The church's strongest position is not simply "historic Sunday worship." It is a historic, inclusive community living room in downtown St. Albans: a place where worship, music, reflection, civic life, volunteer work, support groups, classes, and town celebrations overlap.

The most ownable story is the combination of:

- a church organized in 1803;
- an Eclectic Romanesque red-brick landmark beside Taylor Park;
- a four-faced community clock and bell that are still hand-wound;
- rare Tiffany and Lamb stained-glass windows;
- an 1890s tracker organ and unusually strong musical identity;
- a current Open and Affirming commitment;
- an informal public voice that welcomes "seekers, believers, and doubters."

## Verified current information

### Welcome and faith

- The church is a United Church of Christ congregation and voted to become Open and Affirming on May 16, 2021.
- Its covenant explicitly welcomes people across race, ethnicity, age, sexual orientation, gender identity and expression, family structure, faith background, ability, and economic circumstance, with full participation in leadership, ministry, fellowship, worship, and sacraments.
- The site describes love as its overarching creed, supports open communion, calls the church to service and prophetic public witness, and endorses peaceful/nonviolent solutions.

### Music, building, and ongoing uses

- Choir normally supports worship from September through mid-June and welcomes singers who learn by reading or by ear.
- The church participates in the free Church Street Sounds of the Season concert series and hosts classical, folk, vocal, and informal/open-mic music.
- The sanctuary contains a historic Hook & Hastings/Hale & Alexander tracker organ and a grand piano.
- Public building-use information lists a 350-seat sanctuary, a small library, a roughly 50-person fellowship hall/chapel, a commercial-scale kitchen, and a parlor for approximately 20.
- The building and grounds are described as smoke-free and alcohol-free; rental fees vary with heat/light use, and renters handle setup, cleanup, and security.
- Public updates describe uses including piano lessons, Girl Scouts, youth/community choirs, AA, counseling/support work, plays, exhibits, open mics, community meetings, art activities, and mindful-awareness sessions.

Sources: [Music](https://firstcongregationalstalbans.org/music/), [Building Use](https://firstcongregationalstalbans.org/building-use/), and [Who Uses the Building?](https://mailchi.mp/firstcongregationalstalbans/who-uses-the-building).

## Historical and visual DNA

- The congregation was organized January 2, 1803 as the First Church of Christ.
- The current building followed an 1891 fire and was erected during 1892-1894. Architect R. H. Robertson designed it in an Eclectic Romanesque style with brick and terra-cotta detailing.
- The building is listed on Vermont and federal historic registers.
- The four-faced clock was installed in 1894. Its bell and chime served the city as a community timepiece and historic fire/death signal. A local team continues to climb the tower and wind it weekly.
- The sanctuary includes rare Tiffany memorial windows and Lamb Studio windows; the glass, jewel details, leaf/sky colors, handmade rosettes, and the fingerprints visible in one choir-loft window offer rich pattern inspiration.
- The building is described as a product of "many hands in work and love" - a stronger brand truth than grand institutional language.

Sources: archived historical PDFs in `source-scrape/assets/documents/firstcongregationalstalbans.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/`, plus [SAH Archipedia](https://sah-archipedia.org/buildings/VT-01-FR31).

## Content contradictions and launch checks

These should be resolved with church leadership before a public launch:

1. **Office hours:** the home page says Tuesday-Friday, 9:00 am-2:00 pm; the footer says open daily; Contact says daily but varied.
2. **Capacity:** the building page says 350 seats; one historical image filename says 380. Use 350 until confirmed.
3. **Music contact:** the current staff page lists Bailey Brown, but the music page still names Erin Grainger and an older email address.
4. **Pastor hours:** Staff says Friday, 9:00 am-2:00 pm; the latest bulletin says Friday, 9:30 am-2:30 pm.
5. **Administrator title:** Staff says Church Administrator; the current bulletin uses Office Manager.
6. **Historic dates:** source copy variously associates the organ/building/clock with 1892, 1893, 1894, and one filename with 1903. The history should be copy-edited against primary records.
7. **Children's programming:** nursery arrangements and a "Time with Children" are described, while Sunday School is currently "emerging." Do not market a full weekly children’s program until leadership confirms it.
8. **Safety language:** an August 2026 pastoral letter publicly discusses a departing daycare and cited safety concerns. Any family-program or building-safety claims need explicit leadership approval.
9. **Announcements:** events are split among a WordPress page, Facebook, bulletins, and Mailchimp; old flyers remain in the media library. The formerly indexed `/events/` page now returns 404.
10. **Visitor logistics:** the sources do not state parking capacity, the accessible route, restroom details, childcare ages/policy, or livestream instructions.
11. **Historical editing:** one historical PDF contains dated and offensive terminology about Indigenous people, old membership counts, and other assertions that need historian/editorial review rather than verbatim migration.
12. **Donation:** the current site links successfully to Givelify. Preserve hosted secure checkout initially and verify the account, funds, fees, recurring options, and receipt behavior before redesigning the flow.
13. **Photo consent and accessibility:** confirm rights and current consent practices before republishing identifiable people, particularly children. Most current image alt text is a filename or generic label and must be rewritten.

## Current visual implementation

- Legacy WordPress Twenty Thirteen theme.
- Alegreya headings with Quattrocento Sans body text.
- Slate/navy/pale-blue interface colors.
- A 1600×230 dove/sun/green-hills header graphic with text baked into the image, which weakens responsive behavior and accessibility.
- Church-photo site icon and a separately hosted UCC rainbow affiliation mark.

These elements contain useful history and inclusion cues, but they do not yet form a flexible identity system.

## Recommended content system

Use Mailchimp's public RSS feed as the dependable source for native `What's Happening` and `Reflections` cards. It already publishes daily `Morning Reflection` items and weekly `From the Manse` updates. Curate selected Facebook photos/videos manually and link to the public page rather than using an unfiltered embed or expiring Facebook CDN URLs.

Suggested top-level navigation for the later build:

- Visit
- Worship
- Programs
- Events
- Stories & Reflections
- About
- Give

Suggested home-page priorities:

1. Sunday time, location, and welcoming promise.
2. Plan a Visit / Save a Welcome.
3. This week and coming events.

5. Authentic photo stories.
6. History of the clock, windows, organ, and building.
7. Reflections and Facebook highlights.
